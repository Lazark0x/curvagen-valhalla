# Loop-Route Generation Without Spikes, Lollipops, or Road Reuse: Algorithm Families and Fork Fit

- **Date:** 2026-07-13 (wayfinder ticket [#46](https://github.com/Lazark0x/curvagen/issues/46))
- **Scope:** what algorithm families modern route products (GraphHopper `round_trip`, Kurviger) and the academic literature use to generate distance-constrained loops that avoid (a) **spikes** — out-and-back stubs, (b) **lollipops** — sub-loops hung off the main circuit, (c) **road reuse** — any road ridden twice; and which techniques fit Valhalla's tiled graph and our fork's native thor round-trip action (`curvature-costing`, HEAD `1ac9231f1`) under prod constraints: 3.7 GB Hetzner box, Serbia tiles, 20–300 km (300 clamp), K=12 bank fill live, p95 engine ~2.4 s at 300 km.
- **Method:** primary sources only — GraphHopper master Java source read from GitHub; Kurviger's official API doc + knowledgebase; four papers read as PDFs (Abraham et al. SEA 2010, Zündorf's KIT thesis underlying Gemsa et al. SEA 2013, Paraskevopoulos & Zaroliagis ATMOS 2013, Lewis & Corcoran SN CS 2024); Valhalla upstream docs + our fork source cited `path:line`. Pipeline ground truth reuses [2026-07-12-candidate-banking-feasibility.md](./2026-07-12-candidate-banking-feasibility.md) §1 and was re-verified against HEAD (the min-separation guard and the 1..16 clamp have landed since that doc).

## TL;DR verdict

**Everything in production and almost everything in the literature is one family: via-point loops closed by penalized shortest paths — exactly what our fork already is.** GraphHopper projects 2+ via points on a circle and re-routes legs under a flat **5× penalty** on previously-used edges ([RoundTripRouting.java](https://github.com/graphhopper/graphhopper/blob/master/core/src/main/java/com/graphhopper/routing/RoundTripRouting.java)); Kurviger is that plus curvature weighting ([API doc](https://github.com/boldtrn/kurviger-api-documentation)); the SEA 2013 jogging-routes partial-shortest-paths algorithms are the same idea with **structural** anti-reuse (delete leg-1 nodes from the graph before routing leg 2 — thesis p. 28); the penalty method of the alternatives literature is our leash with **graded rejoin penalties** (ATMOS 2013 p. 113–116). The one genuinely different family — **greedy faces** (grow the loop from adjacent planar faces; sharing ≡ 0 by construction, thesis p. 19) — is the only method that structurally eliminates all three defect classes at once, and it does **not** fit Valhalla's directed, non-planar, tiled graph (new planarization pass + undirected assumption + length overshoot bounded only by the largest face perimeter). Key correction to the ticket's suspicion: our return leg is **not** penalty-free — it seeds a soft reuse leash (both directions, `route_action.cc:1130-1145`) at prod strength 4.2× (`costing.rs:36` × `kReuseStrength=4`, `motorcyclecost.cc:60`) — but the leash is soft (measured residual edge_reuse 0.108, ADR-0033) and the **turnaround neighborhood is structurally unguarded**: `correlate_node` offers the return leg the opposing edge of the forward leg's last edge (`route_action.cc:1017-1026`), so every loop may legally open with a U-turn spike. **Shortlist (details at the end): (1) turnaround hardening — junction-only turnarounds + stub walk-back + drop the U-turn edge from the return origin (harvest, ~zero cost, kills the spike class); (2) hard-exclude the forward edges on the return leg via upstream `user_exclude_edges_` with soft-leash fallback retry (return leg, ~zero cost on success, structurally kills reuse+stem); (3) graded rejoin penalties on the leash (ATMOS 2013 recipe, ~zero cost, shrinks lollipops); (4) K×K sharing filter reusing `alternates.cc` logic (per-expansion post-pass, ms, fixes bank distinctness); (5) optional second via point on the return (TW3-triangle) only if the lollipop KPI stays bad — the only item with real latency (+1 bidir A* per candidate).** Greedy faces and a GraphHopper-style multi-via rewrite are rejected for fit.

## 1. Ground truth: the fork's pipeline and where each defect actually enters

Pipeline (one forward `Dijkstras` expansion under curvy motorcycle costing → harvest → dedup/bucket → per-candidate return leg) is documented with measurements in the [banking feasibility doc §1](./2026-07-12-candidate-banking-feasibility.md); deltas at HEAD `1ac9231f1`:

- Harvest band ±18 % of target/2 (`src/thor/roundtrip_expansion.cc:16`), near-start reject `kMinStraightFraction = 0.3` — explicitly commented as anti "there-and-back … also disfavors near-start lollipops" (`roundtrip_expansion.cc:17-20`, check at `:107`).
- **Min-separation guard landed** (ADR-0036 T9): any turnaround within `0.1 × target/2` straight-line of an already-chosen one is rejected, in both the per-sector pick and the backfill (`src/thor/route_action.cc:1076-1088,1103-1109,1120-1122`).
- **`num_candidates` clamp landed**: 1..16 at parse (`src/worker.cc:1304-1308`).
- **Return leg leash exists** (ticket suspicion outdated): `route_return` marks every forward-leg edge *and its opposing edge* used before the bidirectional A* (`route_action.cc:1130-1145`, marking at `:1132-1140`); `EdgeFactor` multiplies a re-ridden edge's cost by `reuse_factor_` (`valhalla/sif/dynamiccost.h:1262-1266`), where `reuse_factor_ = 1 + reuse_penalty × 4.0` (`src/sif/motorcyclecost.cc:59-60,385-386`). Prod sends `reuse_penalty: 0.8` (`Backend/orchestrator/crates/domain/src/costing.rs:36`) → **4.2× on reuse, soft**. Measured residual: corpus round-trip edge_reuse **0.108** ([ADR-0033](../adr/0033-native-round-trip-thor-action.md) header), i.e. the leash reduces but does not eliminate.
- One-shot distance correction deliberately absent (it converged distinct candidates onto one loop — comment at `route_action.cc:1181-1182`, trade recorded in ADR-0033).

**Where defects structurally enter.** Both legs are individually shortest paths under their (positive-cost) costing — the forward leg is a predecessor-chain walk of the Dijkstra tree (`route_action.cc:979-989`), the return a bidir-A* optimum — and a shortest path with positive edge costs never contains a visit-and-return sub-tour, so *in-leg* spikes/sub-loops are essentially impossible (the same argument as local optimality of via-path halves in Abraham et al., [SEA 2010 PDF](https://renatowerneck.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/adgw10-alternatives-sea.pdf) §4: a via path's "local optimality … can only be violated around v"). Our loop **is** a degenerate via path with s = t and v = turnaround, so all three defect classes are **leg-pairing phenomena concentrated at the seams**:

1. **Spike (a)** — at the turnaround. `correlate_node` builds the return origin from *every* auto-accessible outbound edge **plus its opposing inbound edge** (`route_action.cc:1003-1026`), including the opposing edge of the forward leg's final edge, so the return may open with a straight U-turn (leash-penalized 4.2×, never forbidden). If the harvested label sits mid-spur or in a dead-end tree, *every* exit retraces the spur — the pathological end of this class was #44's zero-exit one-way sink (now skipped, `route_action.cc:1161-1165`).
2. **Reuse (c)** — anywhere the 4.2× soft multiplier loses to sparsity (only road home) or is offset by the curvy discount on attractive edges; residual 0.108.
3. **Lollipop (b)** — the return rejoins the outbound corridor early and shadows it home (on the same edges → also reuse; or on parallel streets → pure shape defect). `kMinStraightFraction` kills the degenerate all-stem case; nothing addresses stem-sharing mid-loop.

History: post-hoc spur surgery was tried and rejected (mutating geometry, blind to lollipops — [ADR-0030](../adr/0030-unified-candidate-pipeline-corridor-exclusion.md) "Why"); per-leg `exclude_polygons` corridors were measured to be slower and to *induce* rings ([ADR-0031](../adr/0031-seed-route-rank-isochrone-loops.md) note in ADR-0030 header); the soft leash came from ADR-0031, the native action from [ADR-0032](../adr/0032-engine-first-route-generation.md)/[ADR-0033](../adr/0033-native-round-trip-thor-action.md), which explicitly rejected hard-excluding the outbound ("risks 'no route home' in sparse mountain networks") and left "lollipop rate … a measured KPI, not separately engineered".

## 2. GraphHopper `round_trip` — the production via-point reference

Read from master (`f866a9c`), [core/src/main/java/com/graphhopper/routing/RoundTripRouting.java](https://github.com/graphhopper/graphhopper/blob/master/core/src/main/java/com/graphhopper/routing/RoundTripRouting.java); feature shipped in 0.7, credited to boldtrn ([GraphHopper 0.7 release post](https://www.graphhopper.com/blog/2016/06/15/graphhopper-routing-engine-0-7-released/)).

- **Via-point generation.** `roundTripPointCount = min(20, POINTS_param default 2 + distance/50 km)` (`Params` ctor) — e.g. 4 points at 100 km, 8 at 300 km. [`MultiPointTour`](https://github.com/graphhopper/graphhopper/blob/master/core/src/main/java/com/graphhopper/routing/util/tour/MultiPointTour.java) generates `allPoints − 1` via points: initial heading = request heading or `random.nextInt(360)`; **heading for iteration i = `initialHeading + 360·i/allPoints`** (evenly spaced compass sectors — the ancestor of our bearing buckets); per-hop distance = `overallDistance/(allPoints+1)` jittered ±10 % ([`TourStrategy.slightlyModifyDistance`](https://github.com/graphhopper/graphhopper/blob/master/core/src/main/java/com/graphhopper/routing/util/tour/TourStrategy.java)). Each point is a **geometric projection** from the previous point (`projectCoordinate(last, distance, heading)`), snapped to the graph; on snap failure the distance shrinks ×0.95 up to `maxRetries` then errors (`generateValidPoint`).
- **Anti-reuse across legs.** `RoundTripCalculator` wraps the weighting in [`AvoidEdgesWeighting`](https://github.com/graphhopper/graphhopper/blob/master/core/src/main/java/com/graphhopper/routing/weighting/AvoidEdgesWeighting.java) with `setEdgePenaltyFactor(5)`; after each leg, that leg's edges join `previousEdges` — i.e. a **flat soft 5× multiplicative penalty on any previously-used edge** (`calcEdgeWeight`: `weight * edgePenaltyFactor` if avoided). Source comment: "add the edges of this path to the set of previous edges so they will be avoided from now, otherwise we do not get a nice 'round trip'. note that for this reason we cannot use CH for round-trips currently."
- **Documented weaknesses (from the source itself).** (i) Distance control is a geometry heuristic (circle circumference + jitter), no on-target guarantee; (ii) the 5× penalty is soft — reuse is reduced, never forbidden; (iii) the code acknowledges **route tails at via points — GraphHopper's own spike class**: "instead getClosestNode (which might be a virtual one and introducing unnecessary tails of the route) use next tower node … Later: remove potential route tail" (`calcPaths`), plus "todo: no snap preventions for round trip so far" (`lookup`); (iv) legs are penalized against *all* previous legs but each leg is otherwise independent — nothing prevents a leg from shadowing another on parallel streets (lollipop class). Independent quality datum: Lewis & Corcoran measured OpenRouteService's `round_trip` (ORS is a GraphHopper-derived engine — [github.com/GIScience/openrouteservice](https://github.com/GIScience/openrouteservice); GH-lineage not independently re-verified here) at **8–15 % mean distance deviation** vs 2–8 % for their local search ([SN CS 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42979-024-03223-3)).

**Take:** identical family to ours, weaker on every axis we care about — no curvature-aware turnaround choice (blind geometric projection), flat penalty (ours is tunable + both-direction seeding), no structural spike guard (they have a TODO where we have #44's fix + this ticket).

## 3. Kurviger — the closest competitor

Primary sources: Kurviger's official [API documentation](https://github.com/boldtrn/kurviger-api-documentation) (boldtrn = Robin Boldt, who also authored GraphHopper's `MultiPointTour`/`TourStrategy`/`AvoidEdgesWeighting` — `@author Robin Boldt` tags in those files — and GH 0.7's round trip) and the [Kurviger knowledgebase](https://docs.kurviger.com/general/definitions).

- Kurviger's API **is** the GraphHopper API with a curvature weighting: "`weighting`: … `curvature`, which calculates a curvy and fun route optimized for motorcyclists"; round trips take `round_trip.distance` (**max 300 000 m** — same clamp as ours), `round_trip.seed` ("results … always the same for the same seed"), and `heading`. `ch.disable: true` — "Kurviger does not support CH" (consistent with GH's round-trip/CH incompatibility above). ([API doc README](https://github.com/boldtrn/kurviger-api-documentation))
- **Anti-reuse is the same soft penalty, exposed as an option**: "`additional_weighting` … For roundtrips you should pass `avoid_edges` to reduce the chance of taking the same road twice. This will avoid taking the same road twice" ([API doc README](https://github.com/boldtrn/kurviger-api-documentation)) — `avoid_edges` is exactly GH's `AvoidEdgesWeighting` name (`getName()` returns `"avoid_edges"`). User-facing: "**Using the same road twice** — Avoids routes that use the same road more than once", strength customizable in paid tiers ([knowledgebase](https://docs.kurviger.com/general/definitions)). Note their own phrasing wobbles between "reduce the chance" and "will avoid" — it is the soft 5× family, not a guarantee.
- Curviness tiers are costing presets ("Fast and curvy" / "Curvy" / "Extra curvy…prefers even more twisty roads and avoids cities even more" — [knowledgebase](https://docs.kurviger.com/general/definitions)), i.e. curvature lives in the weighting, round-trip shape lives in the GH via-point machinery.
- **UNVERIFIED:** anything beyond the public docs — the Kurviger GraphHopper fork is private; forum threads checked ([round-trip-in-a-point](https://forum.kurviger.com/t/round-trip-in-a-point/2692), [automatic-round-trip-generator](https://forum.kurviger.com/t/new-feature-idea-automatic-round-trip-generator/26299)) contain no dev statements on internals. Treat "Kurviger = GH round_trip + curvature weighting + avoid_edges, 300 km cap, seed, heading" as the complete publicly-known picture.

## 4. Academic family A — cycle construction: greedy faces & fixed-length round trips

**Gemsa, Pajor, Wagner, Zündorf, "Efficient Computation of Jogging Routes" (SEA 2013, LNCS 7933, pp. 272–283)** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-38527-8_25); the KIT PDF link on the [authors' publication page](https://i11www.iti.kit.edu/en/members/thomas_pajor/publications) is dead (404), so paper-level claims below rest on the Springer abstract, on the full-detail **primary underlying thesis** (Zündorf, ["Effiziente Berechnung guter Joggingrouten", KIT 2012](https://i11www.iti.kit.edu/_media/teaching/theses/ba-zuendorf-12.pdf) — read in full for the algorithm chapters, page cites below), and on Lewis & Corcoran's peer-reviewed characterization. Problem: cycle through s with length in `[(1−ε)l, (1+ε)l]` optimizing soft criteria (thesis Def. 4.1, p. 13); **NP-hard "even if the only optimization goal is length"** (Springer abstract; thesis ch. 3). Criteria: **sharing(P) = fraction of path length on edges contained more than once in P** — their formalization of our reuse metric — and length-weighted per-edge badness (thesis p. 14).

- **Greedy Faces** (thesis §4.2, pp. 14–24): planarize the street graph (bridges handled by a dual-graph union construction, p. 15), build the dual graph of **faces** ("city blocks"); start from a face at s and repeatedly add an adjacent face (rejecting adds that create holes or drop s from the boundary — the FaceSet structure, pp. 18–19) until the surrounding path reaches l. **Structural guarantee: "the computed path — provided s does not lie in a dead end — is in any case node- and edge-disjoint, i.e. has a Sharing of 0"** (p. 19, translated). Dead-end trees are *skipped during face tracing* precisely because "entering a dead end inevitably leads to sub-sections that must be run twice" (p. 16, translated) — i.e. the face boundary can never contain a spike, a lollipop, or reuse **by construction**. Costs: length overshoot bounded only by the longest face perimeter (p. 19); O(m²) worst-case; needs force/shape steering (Coulomb-like force field + isoperimetric `shape(f) = 2√(area·π)/perimeter`, Alg. 4.2 + Eq. 4.1, pp. 20–22) to avoid ugly local optima; and their own smoothing post-pass **reintroduces sharing > 0** (p. 23).
- **Partial Shortest Paths / TW3–TW4–TWB** (thesis §4.3, pp. 25–35): approximate the loop by a triangle (TW3): candidate set K = nodes at c-distance ≈ l/3 from s (one Dijkstra under ω(e) = c(e)·b(e)); for each p ∈ K a second Dijkstra; pick (p,q) with total length in the band minimizing badness; loop = SP(s,p)∘SP(p,q)∘SP(q,s) (Alg. 4.3, pp. 26–27; O(n² log n + nm) "very conservative"). **Anti-reuse is structural, not penalized: before routing from candidate p, the nodes of SP(s,p) (except p) are deleted from the graph**, and an end-node check makes the q-legs edge-disjoint (p. 28, "Reduzierung des Sharing") — with the explicit escape hatch that sharing near s must be re-allowed "if the start node lies in a dead end" (p. 27). TW4 adds a fourth waypoint and roots each candidate Dijkstra at the *half-way point* h_p of SP(s,p), restricted to the subtree through p, so paths remain locally shortest *through* the waypoints (p. 29) — the same concern as Abraham's around-v optimality; TWB relaxes the midpoint by a bidirectional meet (pp. 31–32). Alternatives fall out as second-best tuples; candidate loops parallelize trivially (p. 32).

**Lewis & Corcoran, "Fast Algorithms for Computing Fixed-Length Round Trips in Real-World Street Networks" ([SN Computer Science, 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42979-024-03223-3))** — the modern follow-up, and the closest academic analog of *our* architecture. k-length round trip (KRT): closed walks minimizing f₁ = |k − L(S)| and **f₂ = "percentage of overall round trip involving travelling along street segments (in either direction) that have already been visited"** (their reuse-in-either-direction metric matches our leash's both-direction marking); NP-hard; **directed graphs** (they call out Gemsa et al. as "limited to undirected graphs; cannot model one-way streets"). Methods: (i) **isochrone-polygon** seeding — k/2 isochrone from s, inscribe n-vertex polygons, link vertices by shortest-path calls (≈ our ADR-0031 ancestor and today's expansion-band + bearing buckets); (ii) **out-and-back removal by iteratively deleting degree-one vertices** of the candidate's undirected route graph — a *structural spike eliminator*; (iii) Pareto local search over the subgraph of streets used by candidates, neighborhoods built from trees "with original arcs removed" — steering toward edge-disjoint alternatives; 96 % of runs < 6 s, 2–8 % length deviation vs ORS's 8–15 %. Related: Stroobant, Audenaert, Colle, Pickavet, ["Generating constrained length personalized bicycle tours" (4OR, 2018)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10288-018-0371-9) — same constrained-length loop problem, NP-hardness + branch-and-bound bound + heuristic (abstract only; method detail not extracted — UNVERIFIED beyond that). ADR-0033 already rejected the from-scratch arc-orienteering solver lane as disproportionate ("Option C").

## 5. Academic family B — alternatives machinery as building blocks

**Via-node alternatives — Abraham, Delling, Goldberg, Werneck, "Alternative Routes in Road Networks" ([SEA 2010, PDF](https://renatowerneck.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/adgw10-alternatives-sea.pdf))**, read in full. An s–t path P is an **admissible alternative** iff (1) `ℓ(Opt ∩ P) ≤ γ·ℓ(Opt)` (limited sharing), (2) P is T-locally optimal for `T = α·ℓ(Opt)`, (3) P is (1+ε)-UBS — every subpath has stretch ≤ 1+ε (§3, with Fig. 1 showing why sharing alone admits ugly near-U-turn detours). Practical algorithm: enumerate **single via paths** P_v = SP(s,v)∘SP(v,t) from the two bidirectional-Dijkstra trees; check sharing in O(n) by tree scans; approximate local optimality by the **T-test** (one P2P query per candidate; passes ⇒ T-LO, fails ⇒ not 2T-LO — Lemmas 1–2); rank candidates by `f(v) = 2ℓ(v) + σ(v) − pl(v)` where pl(v) is the longest **plateau** through v (§6). Experiments on Europe (18 M nodes): ε=25 %, γ=80 %, α=25 %; first alternative found in 91.3 % of queries in 20.4 ms (x-REV), "4 to 12 times slower than a simple P2P query" (§7). The **plateau method** (CAMVIT "Choice Routing", 2005 — original whitepaper cited as their ref [2] at camvit.com, not independently retrievable — UNVERIFIED beyond secondhand descriptions): plateaus = maximal paths in *both* the forward tree from s and backward tree to t; a plateau u–w yields the path s→u→w→t, and "if P corresponds to a plateau v–w, P is dist(v,w)-LO" (Lemma 4). Formal plateau definition: `∀u,v ∈ P̄ : d_s(u)+d_t(u) = d_s(v)+d_t(v)`, ranked by `w(P_st) − w(P̄)` ([ATMOS 2013](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/01oasics/oasics-vol033-atmos2013/OASIcs.ATMOS.2013.108/OASIcs.ATMOS.2013.108.pdf) §3).

**Penalty method — Bader, Dees, Geisberger, Sanders, "Alternative Route Graphs in Road Networks" ([TAPAS 2011](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-19754-3_5), first practical implementation; abstract-level only here) as refined by Paraskevopoulos & Zaroliagis, "Improved Alternative Route Planning" ([ATMOS 2013, open PDF](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/01oasics/oasics-vol033-atmos2013/OASIcs.ATMOS.2013.108/OASIcs.ATMOS.2013.108.pdf), DOI 10.4230/OASIcs.ATMOS.2013.108)**, read in full (pp. 113–116):

- Loop: compute shortest path, penalize its edges, re-query, keep the new path if "short and different enough", repeat until enough alternatives or no improvement (p. 113).
- Weight policy: **`w_new(e) = w(e) + p·w_old(e)` with penalty factor `0.1 ≤ p ≤ 1`** — proportional, "the use of constant values is avoided, because they do not always guarantee a balanced adjustment"; unbounded repeated increases are dangerous ("only one fast highway into a city … any possible alternative inside the city will be lost"), so increases per edge are capped (p. 113).
- **Rejoin penalty**: edges "that leave and join the current AG should be additionally penalized … by a factor `0.1 ≤ r ≤ 1`", refined to a graded form `w_new(e) = w(e) + (0.1 + r·d_s(u)/d_s(t))·w_old(e)` on edges leaving the path (mirrored for entering), "to reduce the possibility of recomputing alternative paths that tend to rejoin directly with the previous one traced" (pp. 114, 116) — i.e. the literature's direct treatment of our **lollipop-stem / corridor-shadowing** defect.
- Quality frame: alternative-graph metrics `totalDistance` (non-overlap), `averageDistance` (stretch), `decisionEdges` (complexity); optimizing any two together is NP-hard; **Plateau and Penalty are the two best methods**, k-shortest-paths and Pareto produce low-quality graphs (p. 109, citing Bader et al.). Penalty needs several full re-queries → they bound it with a bidirectional pruner keeping only nodes with `d_s(v)+d_t(v) ≤ τ·d_s(t)`, `1 ≤ τ ≤ 1.4` (p. 114).

**Mapping to the defect classes:** via-node/plateau give *diversity* and *local optimality* (no small detours ⇒ no spikes inside a candidate) but say nothing about a loop reusing itself — for loops they degenerate (s = t). The penalty method is the reuse tool; its literature form ≈ our leash + two upgrades we lack: **graded rejoin penalties** and **per-edge increase caps**. K-shortest-paths is explicitly documented as the wrong tool ("share many edges", p. 112) — matches ADR-0027/0030's rejection of alternates-as-shape-variety for One-Way.

## 6. Valhalla machinery available to a fork

Upstream (all present in our fork, = upstream 3.7.0 + curvature commits):

- **Hard per-request edge exclusion exists end-to-end**: `exclude_locations` "are mapped to the closest road or roads and these roads are excluded from the route path computation"; `exclude_polygons` "roads intersecting these rings will be avoided during path finding" ([Valhalla API reference](https://valhalla.github.io/valhalla/api/turn-by-turn/api-reference/); fork copy `docs/docs/api/turn-by-turn/api-reference.md:364-365`). Internally both funnel into costing-option `exclude_edges` (loki populates them: `src/loki/worker.cc:164-203`) → `user_exclude_edges_` map (`valhalla/sif/dynamiccost.h:1329`, parse `src/sif/dynamiccost.cc:226-234`, programmatic hook `AddUserAvoidEdges` `dynamiccost.cc:402-405` / `dynamiccost.h:1048-1060`) → **hard reject in each costing's `Allowed()`/`AllowedReverse()`** (`src/sif/motorcyclecost.cc:413-415,438-441`). Service limits: `max_exclude_locations: 50`, `max_exclude_polygons_length: 10 000 m` perimeter (`scripts/valhalla_build_config:402,408`) — limits apply to the *HTTP surface*; an in-fork call to `AddUserAvoidEdges`/a direct set insert has no such cap. **This is the ready-made hard-exclusion channel the shortlist item 2 rides.**
- **Soft per-request penalty set** is our fork's addition: `used_edges_` + `mark_edges_used`/`clear_used_edges` + `EdgeFactor` multiplier (`dynamiccost.h:1262-1280,1416-1417`) — i.e. the penalty-method primitive already lives at the right layer (costing), reusable by any thor algorithm.
- **Bidirectional A* + alternates**: upstream `alternates` ("A number denoting how many alternate routes should be provided … may be no alternates or less" — [API reference](https://valhalla.github.io/valhalla/api/turn-by-turn/api-reference/), default cap `max_alternates: 2`, `scripts/valhalla_build_config:407`) enumerates candidate connections at bidir meeting points and filters them with **Kobitzsch-style viability tests** — the fork file says it verbatim: "Viability tests for alternate paths based on M. Kobitzsch's Alternative Route Techniques (2015). Tests verify limited sharing between segments, bounded stretch, and local optimality" (`src/thor/alternates.cc:9-14`); stretch threshold 1.25–2× (`:17-20,46-65`), sharing threshold 0.6→0.75 by distance (`:21,27-44`), **local optimality "[TODO] NOT IMPLEMENTED"** (`:194-197`). `validate_alternate_by_sharing` (`:150-192`) is a drop-in pairwise loop-overlap filter (edge-id set + shared-length fraction) — exactly the geometric dedup the banking doc asked the orchestrator to do, available engine-side.
- **U-turn handling**: motorcycle transition costs carry a reverse-turn factor `kTCReverse = 9.5` (`src/sif/motorcyclecost.cc:41,47-50`), and the code deliberately relaxes internal-edge U-turn penalties for motorcycles (`:595`, `dynamiccost.h:871-872` "motor_scooter and motorcycle costing models do not penalize" pencil-point U-turns) — but none of this spans the **leg boundary** at our turnaround: each leg is an independent `GetBestPath`, so the loop's U-turn at the turnaround is invisible to transition costing. Structural fix must live in `correlate_node`/harvest, not in the costing.
- **No planar-face machinery exists** anywhere in baldr/mjolnir: tiles store directed edges + nodes in a 3-level hierarchy; there is no embedding, no face enumeration, and one-ways/bridges break the undirected-planar assumptions Greedy Faces needs (Lewis & Corcoran's criticism of Gemsa et al. applies to Valhalla's graph verbatim).

## 7. Fit assessment against prod constraints

Constraints recap: 3.7 GB box, 20–300 km loops, K=12 in one expansion (`fill_k`, `Backend/orchestrator/crates/api/src/cache_layer.rs:461`), engine p95 ~2.4 s at 300 km / K=3, ~3.3 s at K=12 local (banking doc §3), expansion = the dominant shared cost (~50 MB labels), each return leg ~39–88 ms marginal.

| Family | Fit verdict |
|---|---|
| **Penalty family upgrades** (rejoin penalties, per-edge caps, hard-exclude escalation) | **Best fit.** All primitives exist at the costing layer; changes are per-candidate, O(forward-path) marking cost, zero new passes. |
| **Via/plateau admissibility machinery** (sharing filter, T-test) | Fits as a **post-pass**: `alternates.cc` sharing logic reusable K×K in the round-trip action; T-test/local-optimality irrelevant per-leg (legs are exact SPs) but the *sharing* metric directly measures reuse & stem. |
| **TW3-style structural exclusion + escape hatch** | Fits the return leg exactly (node/edge deletion ⇒ `user_exclude_edges_`); the thesis's dead-end escape hatch (allow sharing near s) maps to exempting a start-radius and to fallback-retry. |
| **Second via point (TW3 triangle / GH multi-leg)** | Fits mechanically (another `correlate_node` + leashed `GetBestPath`), but each extra leg costs a bidir A* per candidate: ~+90 ms × 12 local at 300 km, ~2–3× on the Hetzner box (banking doc §3 scaling) — the only shortlisted item with real latency. |
| **Greedy Faces** | **Does not fit.** Needs planarization + dual graph (new mjolnir pass + new data structure), assumes undirected traversal (one-ways break it), length overshoot bounded by the largest face perimeter — rural Serbian "faces" (whole massifs ringed by a few roads) are tens of km, destroying ±18 % targeting at 300 km. Structurally perfect, architecturally foreign. |
| **GH-style multi-via rewrite** | Rejected as a *replacement*: blind geometric via points give up the fork's core novelty (curvy-frontier turnaround selection) and measurably weaker distance control (§2). |
| **Local-search polishing (Lewis & Corcoran LS)** | Defensible offline/eval tool; per-request it multiplies path queries (their 2–6 s at ≤50 km is over budget at 300 km × K=12). Not shortlisted for serving. |

## Candidate shortlist

Ranked. "Structural" = the defect class cannot occur where the mechanism applies; "reduces" = probabilistic improvement.

1. **Turnaround hardening (harvest + `correlate_node`)** — three sub-parts, one commit's worth: *(a)* harvest only junction turnarounds — reject/walk back labels whose end node offers no auto-accessible exit other than the opposing edge of the arriving edge (generalizes the #44 sink skip at `route_action.cc:1161-1165` from "zero exits" to "no fresh exit"), walking the predecessor chain until a node with a fresh exit (Lewis & Corcoran's degree-one pruning, applied at the source instead of post-hoc); *(b)* drop the opposing edge of the forward leg's final edge from the return-leg origin built in `correlate_node` (`route_action.cc:1017-1026`) so the loop cannot open with a U-turn; *(c)* keep *(a)*'s walk-back as the fallback when *(b)* empties the origin. **Cost:** O(path) per candidate at harvest, no extra A*; memory nil. **Integration:** harvest + return-leg origin. **Coverage:** spike **structural** (at the turnaround — the only place spikes can arise, §1); lollipop no; reuse marginal.
2. **Hard-exclude the forward leg on the return, soft-leash fallback** — per candidate, feed the forward edges (both directions, minus a start-exemption radius ~1–2 km so the loop can close on the start's access road — the thesis's dead-end escape hatch, p. 27) into `user_exclude_edges_` via `AddUserAvoidEdges` (`src/sif/dynamiccost.cc:402-405`) for the return `GetBestPath`; on `no route` (the ADR-0033 sparse-network fear, and #44's lesson), clear and retry once with today's soft leash, tagging the loop. **Cost:** on success *cheaper* than today (hard `Allowed()` reject prunes the search vs. exploring 4.2×-inflated edges); on fallback +1 bidir A* for that candidate (~90 ms local / ~0.2 s prod); memory = a hash map of ~path-edge count per candidate, trivial vs the 50 MB expansion. **Integration:** return leg. **Coverage:** reuse **structural on success** (residual = logged fallback rate, expected concentrated at hard mountain starts); spike **structural** on the return side; lollipop stem-on-same-edges **structural** (parallel-street shadowing remains). This is the literature-blessed escalation ADR-0033 deferred — now with the retry ladder that removes its "no route home" objection.
3. **Graded rejoin penalties on the leash** — adopt ATMOS 2013 p. 116: additionally mark (at a lower factor) edges that *leave or join* the forward corridor, graded by progress `d_s(u)/d_s(t)`, implemented as a second penalty tier in `EdgeFactor` seeded from neighbors of forward-path nodes. **Cost:** O(path × avg-degree) marking per candidate, no extra A*; nil memory. **Integration:** return leg (costing seed). **Coverage:** lollipop **reduces** (the anti-corridor-hugging tool the literature built for exactly this); reuse reduces further under fallback; spike no. Pairs with 2 (2 handles the same-edge case; 3 handles the parallel-shadow case that hard exclusion cannot see).
4. **K×K loop-sharing filter (engine post-pass)** — reuse `validate_alternate_by_sharing`'s edge-set overlap (`src/thor/alternates.cc:150-192`) across the K built loops before serialization, threshold ~50–60 % per the banking doc §4 measurements; combined with the already-landed min-separation guard it closes the "5/12 byte-identical" bank-distinctness hole engine-side instead of orchestrator-side. **Cost:** per-expansion post-pass, O(K² × path edges) set ops ≈ ms; nil memory beyond K edge-id sets. **Coverage:** none of the three per-loop defects directly — it is the *cross-candidate* reuse filter (bank quality), listed because it shares the machinery and the ticket's downstream consumer (banking) needs it.
5. **Second via point on the return (TW3 triangle) — conditional** — for each candidate, pick a second waypoint from the *same* expansion at ~l/3 path-distance and a bearing offset from the turnaround, route turnaround → via → start with legs 2–3 each excluded/leashed against all prior edges (thesis §4.3.1 + p. 28 discipline). **Cost:** +1 bidir A* per candidate ⇒ ~+1.1 s local / est. +2–3 s prod at 300 km × K=12 — the only item that materially moves p95; can be applied lazily (only to *served* candidates) under banking. **Integration:** new step between harvest and return. **Coverage:** lollipop **structural for the stem** (three mutually-excluded legs cannot fold into a stem+bulb), reuse further reduced, spike unchanged (turnaround count doubles — needs item 1 at both joins). Gate on the lollipop KPI after 1–3 ship; do not build speculatively (ADR-0033: "a measured KPI, not separately engineered").

**Rejected for fit:** Greedy Faces (structurally ideal — sharing ≡ 0 by construction, thesis p. 19 — but requires planarization + undirected faces Valhalla doesn't have, and its length control collapses on rural macro-faces, §7); full GH-style multi-via rewrite (gives up curvature-aware turnaround selection for blind geometry with documented weaker distance control, §2); post-hoc spur surgery (re-litigated and rejected in ADR-0030); per-request `exclude_polygons` corridors (measured to induce rings, ADR-0031 via ADR-0030 header; the 10 km perimeter service limit also makes the HTTP form useless at our scale, `scripts/valhalla_build_config:408`).
